## First principles

SPY options are **American**: the holder may exercise on any day, not only at expiry. That right is worth something, so an American option costs at least as much as its **European** twin. The difference is the **early-exercise premium** (EEP).

Black-76 is a European model ([concept 07](07-black-76-on-the-forward.md)). Fed American mids, it gets two things wrong:
- **the forward**: an American put's premium lowers C − P, so the parity forward F_K = K + (C − P)/DF comes out low, more so as the strike and the rate rise (ADR-0008, Revised, point 3);
- **the IVs**: an American mid solved as European gives too high a σ.

**De-Americanisation** removes the premium from each mid first, so everything downstream is European-equivalent. Measuring the premium needs a model that knows about early exercise: a binomial lattice.

Terms:
- **Lattice**: a tree of possible stock prices. τ is cut into *N* steps of length dt = τ/N; each step the stock goes up by a factor *u* or down by *d*.
- **Risk-neutral probability** *p*: the up-probability under which the stock's expected growth is the risk-free rate.
- **Backward induction**: start from the payoffs at expiry and walk back one step at a time.
- **Continuation value**: what the option is worth at a node if it is NOT exercised there.
- **American IV** σ_A: the σ at which the lattice's American price equals the mid.
- **Ex-date**: the first day the stock trades without its next dividend. It opens lower by about the dividend *D*.

## The maths

**CRR** (Cox, Ross and Rubinstein, 1979): u = e^(σ√dt), d = 1/u, p = (e^(r·dt) − d)/(u − d). After *i* steps with *j* ups the stock is S₀·u^j·d^(i−j). At expiry the option is worth its payoff; one step back it is worth e^(−r·dt)·[p·V_up + (1 − p)·V_down], and an American option is worth the larger of that and what exercising pays at that node.

Worked step (computed with `uv run python -c`, 2026-10-29): σ = 0.20, r = 5%, τ = 30/365, N = 200 give dt = 0.000411 years, u = 1.0040627, d = 0.9959538, p = 0.5015204.

**The guard on p.** *p* is a probability only while d < e^(r·dt) < u, that is while σ > |r|√dt. Below that it leaves (0, 1) and the walk prices nonsense: at σ = 1e-4, 30 days and 5%, an ITM 430 call came out at 10.000 instead of S − K·e^(−rτ) = 11.7635 (M2 review, code claim 3). Every lattice now prices at max(σ, 2|r|√dt, 1e-4), where *p* is about 0.75: 0.0020 at 30 days and 5%, 0.0102 at 700 days and 5.22% (`lattice.sigma_floor`; `tests/test_lattice.py` pins 11.7635).

**Escrowed dividends.** A drop of *D* on the ex-date would stop the tree recombining. The escrowed model moves only the **risky part** S* = S − PV(dividends to come) on the tree, and adds back the dividends still pending at each node: the stock at a node at time *t* is S* plus Σ D·e^(−r(t_D − t)) over the dividends with t_D > t. A dividend exactly at a node's time has gone ex at that node (`tests/test_lattice.py::test_a_dividend_exactly_at_a_node_time_has_gone_ex_at_that_node`).

**When the drop happens.** SPY opens ex-dividend, so the last chance to exercise a call for the dividend is the close of the session before the ex-date, and the stock never trades cum-dividend after that close. The build times each drop just after that close: ((ex_date − quote_date).days − 1)/365 + 1e-9 (`src/volbt/pricing/dividends.py:144`). Timed at the ex-date itself, the lattice let a cum-dividend stock diffuse for a whole extra day, with an exercise decision at every step: on 2023-03-16, the eve of an ex-date that was also the expiry, the out-of-the-money 400 call (mid $0.335, S = 396.11) carries $0.181 of premium at its own American IV and $0.256 at the build's IV with the drop at the ex-date, against $0.000 with the drop after the close (`uv run python tools/report_m2.py`, block "the dividend drop's timing"; the first run's "$0.33" is not reproduced: it was measured under the σ₀ rule and the last paid amount). On the test's stylised case the premium with the drop after the close is under 1e-6 (`tests/test_forward.py`).

**The premium on one lattice.** EEP = American − European, both priced on the SAME lattice. Most of the lattice's own discretisation error is common to both and cancels, so N = 200 is enough for the premium even where it is not for the price. The European price needs no backward walk: it is e^(−rτ)·Σ C(N, j)·p^j·(1−p)^(N−j)·payoff_j.

**At which σ.** The premium depends on σ, and the mid's own σ is not known yet. ADR-0008 §3a first took σ₀, the Black-76 IV of the raw mid. But the raw mid of an American put contains the premium, so σ₀ is too high, the premium priced at it is too large, and the forward built on it too low. That error is first order where the premium is large: on a clean synthetic chain at 700 days, r = 5.22% and σ 0.12 (the pricer's own rate and dividend), σ₀ settled on a forward 101 bp low and flagged 120 of 130 quotes `PARITY_OUTLIER`, with 20 passes or N = 1,000 (M2 review, design claim 3; round-1 measurement).

**The rule now** (ADR-0008 amendment 1, default taken 2026-10-29): each quote's premium is priced at its own **American IV**:

1. σ_A solves CRR_American(σ_A) = mid on the lattice (S, r, τ, the projected dividends), bracketed in [σ_floor, 5]. The solve starts at the Black-76 IV of the mid on the lattice's own forward, moved by the lattice's European discretisation error there (a closed-form sum, no walk), then steps by Newton (Black-76 vega) and secant, bisecting whenever a step would leave the bracket. A whole date's quotes are solved together, one backward walk per iteration (`src/volbt/pricing/deam.py`).
2. σ_A counts only where the price **identifies** it (M2 round 2, ADR-0008 amendment 7). Deep in the money, exercising at once can be optimal at the root for a whole range of σ, so CRR_American(σ) is flat there: any σ in the range "matches" the mid, and the premium at it is not the quote's. So with dA = A(σ_A(1 + 10⁻⁶)) − A(σ_A) and dE the same for the European price, σ_A is accepted only if dA exceeds the solve's price tolerance and dA ≥ dE/2 (then an error ε in the lattice's American price moves the premium by at most ε). A quote that fails is `american_unidentified`, and its premium is priced at its partner's (same expiry and strike, other type) identified American IV instead; with no such partner it has no premium and stays out of the forward.
3. EEP = CRR_American(σ_A) − CRR_European(σ_A); the European-equivalent mid is mid − EEP.
4. The forward is parity on the European-equivalent mids of the 10 strikes nearest K*, call and put: the median F_K over the 5 strikes nearest the new K*.
5. Every OTM quote gets the same premium at its own American IV, and its IV is the Black-76 IV of mid − EEP on that forward.

This is **self-consistent**: the premium is what the lattice gives the quote at the vol that reprices it, and it does not depend on F, so one pass is the whole procedure. A call with no dividend before its expiry is never exercised early (r ≥ 0), so its American price is its European one and its premium is 0 without a walk. A mid outside [A(σ_floor), A(5)] has no American IV: `IV_FAIL` with reason `american_below_floor` or `american_above_cap`, and no premium. So does a quote with no positive mid (`american_no_mid`), τ ≤ 0 (`american_expired`) or a non-finite input, a NaN dividend included (`american_bad_input`, at once, with no walk). If fewer than 3 near-K* pairs have both quotes solved, the forward stays the raw parity forward and says so (`method = parity_median5_raw`, the smile's `SMILE_FORWARD_RAW`). In the full build that happened to 1 OptionsDX expiry within 60 days, 1 beyond 180 days and 2 DoltHub ones (`uv run python tools/report_m2.py`, block "forward against the carry forward").

**Worked example**, ADR-0008 test 8 (`uv run python tools/report_m2.py`, block "ADR-0008 test 8 and its 60-day case", 2026-10-29). An American chain at flat σ = 0.12, S = 442.61, r = 5.22%, τ = 29/365, a $1.638367 dividend tomorrow, priced on a 2,000-step truth lattice; the true forward is 442.8031.
- With de-Americanisation: F is **+0.031 bp** from the truth, maximum OTM IV error **0.0060 vol points**. The ADR's bounds are 0.1 bp and 0.01 vol points.
- The mutant without it: **−4.307 bp**, maximum IV error **0.19 vol points**. The test must fail with the correction removed, and it does.
- Its 60-day case (r = 5%, σ 0.20, a dividend at 30 days): +0.042 bp and 0.0109 vol points, against −7.216 bp and 0.2325 without.

A band in z = k/(σ√τ) does not bound it either: at z = −3 the premium is 0.019 to 0.097 vol points from 90 days to 2 years at r = 5.5% (block "the premium in vol points at z = ...").

**Early exercise itself** (ADR-0003). Calls: exercising before an ex-date beats holding iff D > P_E(K) + K·(1 − e^(−rτ)), with P_E the European put on the ex-dividend stock and τ from the ex-date to expiry; exact when that ex-date is the last before expiry. Puts: a pre-filter K·(1 − e^(−rτ)) > C_E(K) + PV(D), which is necessary only; then a 500-step lattice, exercising iff continuation − (K − S) ≤ $0.005 per share. ADR-0003 test 7 (K = 450, 30 days, σ = 0.20) gives margins **0.5201** (r = 5%, S = 420: not exercised, though the old rule assigned it), **−0.0037** (S = 400: exercised), **1.0986** (S = 395 with a $1.70 dividend in 10 days: waiting for it dominates) and **0.0167** (r = 0.25%, S = 390: not exercised) (`tests/test_exercise.py`). At r = 5% exercise is optimal below S = 409.02; the rule with its $0.005 tolerance fires below 409.75 (block "the 420 put at 5%").

## Assumptions

- **The premium is the lattice's at the quote's own American IV.** One σ per quote, the one that reprices it on the lattice. The lattice's forward is the carry forward (DGS3MO and the projected dividends), not the parity forward, so the American IV of a quote absorbs part of any gap between the two.
- **Dividends at a projected amount, on projected ex-dates.** No look-ahead: the amount is the same quarter a year earlier times the trailing year's growth, from dividends paid by the quote date; the dates follow SPY's third-Friday rule (`src/volbt/pricing/dividends.py`; ADR-0008 amendment 4). The amount matters most on an ex-date eve, where it sets the call premium and so the forward.
- **Exercise only at the close.** The lattice can exercise at any of its steps; the market exercises once a day. For puts the difference is small; for calls before an ex-date it is exactly the case the drop timing above handles.
- **Calendar time.** The lattice diffuses over weekends at the same rate as weekdays (ADR-0008 §5).

## Where it breaks

- **One rate for every tenor, beyond 180 days.** Almost all of OptionsDX's `PARITY_OUTLIER` quotes are beyond 180 days (block "IV flags by tenor"), and the clean chains above give none, so the method on clean quotes is not what flags them. The mechanism (M2 round 2, design claim 4): a deep-ITM quote's premium is priced at its partner's OTM IV on the lattice, whose carry forward runs at DGS3MO with the projected dividends, so its European-equivalent F_K is pinned near that carry forward; beyond 180 days the flag therefore measures the gap between the lattice's carry forward and the market's parity forward, not the quality of the quote. Flagged quotes stay in the smile and the flag changes nothing downstream. With a per-expiry rate at which the lattice's carry forward equals the parity forward, the flagged quotes on the round-2 reviewer's 8 dates fell from 3,600 to 356 of 16,768, and forwards moved by up to +42.5 bp; ADR-0008 amendment 8 proposes it for the owner, and until then the strategies trade at most 180 days. Rates implied by option boxes do sit above Treasury yields of the same tenor (van Binsbergen, Diamond and Grotteria, "Risk-free interest rates", *Journal of Financial Economics* 143(1), 2022). *(The round-1 text here also quoted a parity-implied rate by year, "0.55 points below DGS3MO in 2022": withdrawn, because that regression ran on mids whose premiums were themselves priced at the one rate.)*
- **Deep in the money.** The premium is most of an ITM put's time value, and its American IV is barely pinned by the mid, or not at all where A(σ) is flat: that is why the identification step exists, why only OTM quotes get an IV, and why the ITM side of a parity pair is priced at its partner's OTM IV for the outlier test (`eep_partner`). Some deep-ITM mids are below their exercise value, which no American model prices (block "deep in-the-money call mids below S − K").
- **A wrong dividend.** A projected ex-date a day off, or an amount 10% off, moves the call premium a lot just before the ex-date and hardly at all elsewhere. The largest corrections within 60 days are near-the-money calls 2 or 3 days before an ex-date that is also their expiry: 8.86 vol points on 2017-12-13, the SPY 265.5 call (block "the largest IV corrections").
